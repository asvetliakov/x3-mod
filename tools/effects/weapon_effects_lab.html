What this draws, and what is approximated
    
    
      
  • Each slot plays one Effects row on the shared looping timeline (t = 0 is the spawn): muzzle (TBullets col 21),
        projectile (the col 0 body with the col 19 flight effect attached), impact (col 20) and the death explosion of
        the chosen tier (TShips col 47 by class, TDocks/TFactories col 14, TAsteroids col 8; 0 = default row). The
        Vanilla tab shows the four slots of the vanilla reconstruction; the Shield hit and Hull hit tabs draw ours on the
        stage (renderOurs(record, t), the impact slot), each element switchable in the Layers panel (the
        hull's "glow" is the soft bulb on the hull around the hit, "flash halo" the flash's coloured halo). The layer
        "hull body" draws the preview hull (the same ellipsoid whose depth and normal the elements use) as a dim grey body,
        lambert from the eye at 0.08 to 0.15 display value with a lighter rim, under the elements: a glow, ring or
        scorch that lies on the hull ends at its silhouette, and the body shows where that is (a preview aid: the game
        draws its own hull).
    • 
      
  • An element is its body (numeric id N = objects/v/N, five digits) drawn from its LOD-0 triangles and UVs at
        uniform scale = element size: a body flat in x/y faces the camera; any other body (shockwave discs lie in x/z)
        is drawn as a 3D object seen from 30 degrees above its x/z plane (approximated: the engine orients it
        by the effect's carrier); it lives duration ms from
        timeoffset. Size: GROW min to max linearly; GROWANDSHRINK min, max at half-life, back to min
        (sine, approximated); otherwise max. rndsize and RANDOMSIZE shrink by up to that fraction
        (seeded). FADEOUT / FADEOUTBODY fade linearly to zero; RANDOMROT spins the sprite to a seeded angle. An
        element without TIMEOUT stays for the whole loop. Offsets, CUT, atten, halfspeed, NOZTEST, NOSKIP, WRECK,
        DEBRIS, PUSH and the row flags (NOROTATE, NODRIFT, HIDEOBJECT) are listed but not drawn (unknown or not
        visual here).
    • 
      
  • Blend per material: ONE/ONE as additive (canvas lighter), ONE/INVSRCCOLOR as
        screen (exactly src + dst(1 - src)), SRCALPHA/INVSRCALPHA as source-over with the alpha texture
        (when it is a real file; a texture that falls back to the NONE_* placeholder is ignored as an alpha source).
        Additive colour is the diffuse premultiplied by its own alpha (inferred; most diffuse maps have constant
        alpha). Classic and MATERIAL3 materials (no recorded blend) are drawn additive (approximated).
    • 
      
  • Texture animations (-N names, types/Animations) play their frames, each a sprite-sheet tile by its UV
        offset, evenly over the element's life (TAT_TAGONESHOT: once; frame durations are not read,
        approximated); on the projectile they cycle at 25 frames/s.
    • 
      
  • Projectile: the body as authored (its own crossed-card length and width, i.e. the vanilla elongation of the
        mesh), seen from the side with the flight axis to the right, rolled 30 degrees about it so both cards show.
        The node scale from cols 16-18 and the 0.1 x speed quad length are printed but not applied (their units against
        body units are not traced). The optional tint multiplies by cols 13-15 normalised to the brightest channel; it is off by default because
        these columns create the bullet's light (weapon-effect-spawn.md, cols 13-15) and the bolt atlases carry their own
        colour per tile (stock SS_BULLET_IRE: blue light, orange tile).
    • 
      
  • Every canvas fits its own content (scale printed in the corner), so sizes compare within a slot, not between
        slots. Zoom "fit whole life" keeps one scale for the loop (growth is visible, early frames can be tiny);
        "fit current time" refits every frame to the elements alive at t. LIGHT elements are scene lights in the engine: their body is not drawn (stock rows give them bodies such as
        objects/v/00002, a large mesh); when enabled they show as a faint radial glow of the element size. LENSFLARE elements get a generic streak flare (the flare tables are not read).
        PROPAGATE children spawn n in [min, max] copies of the child row at seeded delays in [min, max] delay after
        prop_delay, scattered over half the parent's extent (approximated). Particle generators and trails
        (Particles3) are not drawn.
    • 
      
  • Scenes. An absent numeric body objects/v/N falls back to objects/cut/N (inferred: the engine's search
        order for effect element bodies is not traced; its cut-scene loader names cut ids cut\%05d). Every such member
        is a scene (text P n; B id; parts with keys, or binary CUT1), as are the asteroid and debris
        bodies some rows name. A scene is drawn as one body: the first key of every part, each part body at its
        first-key position (scene units / 65536, inferred; CUT1 key positions are not decoded, those parts sit at
        the origin), without the key rotation and without the later keys, so the parts do not fly apart; at most 3000
        triangles per scene, shared by its parts.
    • 
      
  • A missing body is a dashed circle of the element size; a missing Effects row is named in the slot.
    • 
      
  • Ours, impact slot (docs/architecture/weapon-effects.md 5.1.1): the shield-hit hex patch on a preview owner (a
        sphere of 15 / 60 / 300 m for the three tiers, its front facing the camera, a narrow hull of half-extents
        1:1:6 seen side on (its flank's middle or 40 % aft) or from above, or a flat fighter of 6:1:4 from above or
        below), with the vanilla impact row drawn over it (the layer "native impact sprite": the native sprite stays in the
        game; its scale here is fitted to the patch footprint, approximated; the "bubble outline" is a preview
        guide, not drawn by the game). The hits land
        on the preview hull, every bolt arriving from the camera, and the patch is laid on the hull as the screen-space
        decal of the DLL: per pixel the hull's point and its normal (the 2x2 quad's differences of the hull depth), per
        face of the six (the owner's +-z, +-x, +-y lattices) its triplanar share (the outward normal's component, sharpened
        and floored), the pixel's cell on that face's lattice, lit when the face's cell set holds it within the extent of
        the cell's anchor hit along the axis. The patch law, the cell hash, the face cell sets (each hit lights the cells
        of its footprint on every face, each cell on the clock and level of the newest hit that lit it), the
        ring of the newest hits and the eviction of the oldest clocks past a face's draws are the HLSL's and the stage's
        (weapon_hex_patch.hlsl, weapon_shell_ps.hlsl, weapon_effects_stage.h) in
        double precision (the GPU's float hash differs below 1e-3).
                Approximated: the edge anti-aliasing uses forward differences per pixel for the GPU's quad
        fwidth; the hull depth is the preview ellipsoid's (the game's is the depth lane); the hull is
        shaded dimly so its shape shows (the layer "hull body"); the radiance is shown as 1 - e-x per channel added to the
        background in linear light (the game goes through its HDR, TAA and tonemap chain); the lifetime follows the
        controls (the DLL uses the header's patch_lifetime_ms); the colour pickers hold the header's linear
        colours as 8-bit sRGB; a region's spill draws are one pass here (the same cells; the GPU splits them into draws
        of PATCH_CELLS). Hits arrive per second of game time (the SETA rate shortens the wall-time period) and are
        scattered within two cells of the aim point; "drain" lowers the shield fraction per hit so a sequence walks the
        colour bands; the newest hit's shield colours the patch. The automatic loop runs to the end of our patch (its
        lifetime / SETA) plus 250 ms, so the patch is not cut at the vanilla sprite's end.
